# Guided focus · one clear next move

One judgment or action is the organizing unit. Context and progress support that moment without competing with it.

## Composition

Make the current distinction dominant through scale, spacing, and deliberate empty space. Keep prerequisites, a short recap, and the next decision quieter. Put the active question and its possible answers where they can be understood together. Equal outcomes should receive equal visual treatment.

The approved example (1060 × 1280) uses a large completion state to unify two research outcomes. It is a static explanation, so it has no simulated wizard controls. In a real workflow, preserve entered state and provide a clear way to revise earlier decisions.

## Visual language

Lavender `#EDE9FA`, ink `#2D2152`, purple `#6D45CF`, white selected surface. Humanist or system sans-serif, generous spacing, strong scale contrast, and restrained soft corners. Fine process lines stay subordinate; the central choice or finding carries the accent. Use depth sparingly.

## Apply it

- **KPIs:** one finding relevant to the current decision, with a comparison or uncertainty close enough to judge it.
- **Design feedback:** one issue, enough context to understand it, and the next review action.
- **Forms:** one coherent question at a time for unfamiliar or branching tasks, with meaningful progress if the sequence is known.
- **Explainers:** one distinction, convergence, or conclusion made visually memorable with sparse supporting context.

Choose this when reducing simultaneous information improves judgment. Practiced repetitive input is often faster in a register or composer. Do not turn every field into a separate step or hide context needed to answer it.
