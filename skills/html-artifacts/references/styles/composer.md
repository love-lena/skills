# Compact working surface · forest-citrus composer

The editable draft is the organizing unit. Content comes first, with compact properties, evidence, and an action close at hand.

## Composition

Put the thing being made in a contained working surface. Keep useful context visible beside or behind it without making the surrounding application chrome the main event. Attach properties and purpose choices to the draft; put validation and save state near the action. A compact surface need not be a modal.

The approved example is wide (1500 × 900) and pictures an experiment brief with an open purpose menu. It is a static concept. A working version needs actual labeled inputs, a semantic select or accessible menu, keyboard behavior, dismissal where relevant, and implemented save behavior.

## Visual language

Forest `#102D29`, paper `#F1F5E8`, ink `#183C34`, citrus `#D7EB53`, quiet text `#536E60`. Readable compact sans-serif, modest corners, closely grouped properties, and shallow depth when surfaces overlap. Citrus identifies the active choice or priority action; keep normal reading surfaces quiet.

## Apply it

- **KPIs:** small relevant measurements inform the draft or decision, with source and period; they remain secondary to the work.
- **Design feedback:** turn observations into an editable request, with attributed evidence and intent beside the text.
- **Fast forms:** sensible defaults, a short predictable tab order, local validation, and one clear save action.
- **Explainers:** an example draft and its available choices make the underlying model concrete.

Choose this for familiar, bounded work. It relies on context and sensible defaults. A substantial unfamiliar intake needs more room than a tiny composer, and a mockup must not imply saved data or approval.
