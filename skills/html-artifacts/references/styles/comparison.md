# Asymmetric comparison · comparison desk

The consequential difference is the organizing unit. Shared criteria and aligned points let the reader compare without remembering separate pages.

## Composition

Allocate space according to the information needed on each side. Compress a repeated dependency or familiar baseline; expand the proposal or differences that need examination. Use equal space when parity is important. Place a recommendation beside the evidence that supports it.

The approved wide example (1740 × 980) uses a narrow serial chain and a large parallel surface. Its unequal areas express the proposal's structure, not measured speed, effort, or a proven winner. On narrow screens, keep paired criteria near each other rather than splitting whole versions far apart.

## Visual language

Paper `#FCFCF5`, black `#111111`, electric blue `#163BDE`, lime `#E4EE62`. Bold sans-serif, strong rules, sharp edges, little depth. Large contrasting regions separate alternatives; aligned baselines and labels tie them together. Reserve lime for a specific active difference or evidence marker.

## Apply it

- **KPIs:** periods or segments aligned by metric and unit, with the difference visible and its interpretation nearby.
- **Design feedback:** versions at comparable scale, annotated where the change occurs; use asymmetry only when it improves inspection fairly.
- **Fast forms:** current configuration beside an editable draft, with changed fields and consequences easy to inspect.
- **Explainers:** two ways of organizing the same work, with shared assumptions and limits explicit.

Choose this for a bounded comparison or decision. It splits attention and is less useful for a single routine action. Schematic length or area needs qualification when it could be mistaken for measured improvement.
