# Visual Explainer

Make the explanation work through pictures. The reader should grasp the main ideas by scanning the visuals and their labels. Combine this method with the selected presentation: Plain by default, or a Fancy composition when requested. It supplies no separate palette or page template.

Start with the distinction or relationship the reader needs to understand. Choose a labeled illustration, sequence, comparison, diagram, small chart, or concrete metaphor that makes that relationship visible. Let visuals carry the explanation; use short headings, direct labels, and brief takeaway captions. Put optional technical depth and sources in disclosure or footnotes while keeping qualifications that change the takeaway visible beside it.

Simplify the representation while preserving meaning. Containment differs from sequence, access from authority, and association from causation. Measured marks need units and appropriate scales. Label schematic counts, spacing, or geometry when a reader might mistake them for measurements. An attractive diagram must not imply evidence or certainty absent from the source.

Use SVG or HTML/CSS when the relationships and labels are the work. Use bitmap illustrations when they explain better. Friendly and approachable does not require a playful theme or generated imagery.

Add interaction when changing a parameter or revealing a step makes the idea easier to understand and suits the requested deliverable. A static report stays static. The explanation should remain understandable in its initial state.

Inspect the actual render at its intended sizes. Check that the visuals carry the point without paragraphs of supporting prose, that labels remain legible, and that accessible descriptions or text equivalents cover the meaningful relationships. For substantial diagrams, also use the [diagram guide](diagrams.md).
