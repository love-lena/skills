A shared style for readable artifacts

Give RFCs and status boards a consistent visual language. Keep the document’s structure, then apply a familiar treatment for type, spacing, color, and diagrams.

One clear reading path

Keep the main content in a single column, including on desktop. Let whitespace and headings separate ideas. Use the wider canvas for a useful diagram or a comparison that benefits from alignment.

On a phone, reflow the page and its diagrams while keeping the text comfortably sized. A smaller screen should change the arrangement, not the amount of information.

Diagrams carry the explanation

Show the abstractions and relationships that matter. Name the connections, make their direction clear, and keep related parts together. Diagram labels should be readable before the surrounding prose is read.

Keep geometry meaningful. Side-by-side elements are useful when their relationship is the point. Independent prose sections should follow one another down the page.

Secondary detail stays available

Use inline footnote links for brief qualifications, definitions, and source notes. Use disclosure for longer optional explanations that benefit from staying beside their context. Keep the central argument and any uncertainty that could change the decision in the main reading flow.
